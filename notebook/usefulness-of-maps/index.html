On the usefulness of maps

A map is powerful precisely because it leaves things out.

October 13, 2026 · 3 min read

A map becomes useful by refusing to reproduce the world in full.

That sounds obvious, but it is a useful principle far beyond cartography: a representation earns its value through selective omission. The interesting question is not whether a model is incomplete. It must be. The question is whether it preserves the right relationships for the job at hand.

“To collect is to pay attention to the world.”
